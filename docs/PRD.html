iLumos — AI Chat-Based Claim Chart Refinement

PRD v1 · Product: iLumos (Lumenci)

Problem Statement

Patent analysts receive AI-drafted claim charts mapping patent claim elements to accused product evidence, but first-pass AI output is frequently too weak, vague, or legally imprecise to submit as-is — evidence may be inferential rather than documentary, reasoning may not anticipate claim construction disputes, and the AI sometimes misses features or cites the wrong source entirely. Today this refinement happens through manual editing, which is slow and doesn't leverage the AI's ability to re-research and re-argue on request. Analysts need a fast, auditable way to iteratively strengthen a claim chart through natural-language requests, with the AI never making an unreviewed change to a document that will be used in legal proceedings.

User Stories

  • As a patent analyst, I want to select a specific claim element and ask the AI to strengthen its evidence, so I don't have to manually re-research and re-write it.
  • As a patent analyst, I want to correct the AI when it cites wrong or inaccurate evidence, so the chart stays factually reliable.
  • As a patent analyst, I want to undo a refinement I disagree with, so I can experiment with AI suggestions without fear of losing the original.
  • As a patent analyst, I want the AI to tell me when it can't find supporting evidence and ask me for a source, rather than fabricating a weak justification.
  • As a patent analyst, I want to set system instructions (e.g. "be conservative on legal claims") once, so the AI's tone and rigor stay consistent across the session.
  • As a patent analyst, I want every AI suggestion to require my explicit accept before it's applied, so nothing enters a legal work product without my review.

Core Features — MVP Scope

In scope
  • Upload claim chart + optional reference docs
  • System instructions box, set once per session
  • Row-scoped chat refinement
  • AI proposes a specific replacement for Evidence or Reasoning
  • Accept / Reject / Modify gate on every proposal
  • Single-step undo per row
  • Confidence flag (strong/moderate/weak), proactively cited by the AI
  • "Cannot find evidence" fallback → request doc/URL
  • Mock export to Word
Out of scope (v1)
  • Real LLM integration (mocked/deterministic for this prototype)
  • Real file parsing (PDF/OCR ingestion)
  • Multi-step undo history / branching versions
  • Multi-user collaboration, comments, or auth
  • Free-form whole-chart edits in one prompt
  • Legally-formatted, citation-grade Word export

Key Decisions

1. Refinement is row-scoped, not chart-wide. A single ambiguous prompt across an entire chart risks the AI silently touching claim elements the analyst didn't intend to change. Scoping each request to one selected element keeps AI reasoning traceable and matches how analysts already think about claim charts (element by element).
2. Every AI suggestion is a proposal, not a write. Given this output feeds legal filings, we treat the AI as a drafting assistant, not an editor with write access. Human-in-the-loop accept/reject/modify is non-negotiable even though it adds a click.
3. The AI proactively flags weak evidence and admits when it can't find support, rather than only responding when asked. LLMs will produce a plausible-sounding justification even from thin evidence if asked to; instructing the AI to flag confidence and refuse ungrounded claims reduces the single biggest legal risk in this workflow (an inference dressed up as documentary evidence).

Acceptance Criteria

  • Given a chart with a "weak" confidence row, when the analyst asks to strengthen it without supplemental docs, the AI must ask for a document/URL rather than fabricate evidence.
  • Given an AI proposal, the chart must remain unchanged until the analyst clicks Accept or Confirm-edit; Reject must leave the chart untouched and prompt for feedback.
  • Given an accepted change, the analyst must be able to revert that specific row to its prior value via Undo, and the chat must confirm what was reverted.
  • Given the analyst states an AI citation is incorrect and supplies the correct quote, the next chart update must reflect the supplied quote, not the original AI evidence.
  • The 3-column chart (Claim Element / Evidence / Reasoning) must be visible and update in place after every accepted change, with no page reload.

Success Metrics

  • Efficiency: median time to bring a claim element from "weak" to "strong" confidence via chat, vs. manual editing baseline.
  • Trust/adoption: % of AI proposals accepted or modified (vs. rejected outright) — a low accept rate signals poor suggestion quality.
  • Safety: % of chat sessions where the analyst issues a correction (edge case 1) — tracked as a quality signal on the underlying evidence-retrieval, not just usage.
  • Undo rate: low undo usage suggests the accept step is doing its job as a meaningful checkpoint rather than a rubber stamp.
